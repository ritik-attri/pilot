# Scenario index

Every file under `scenarios/` is a scenario in the brief's format: one JSON event per line (`start`, `msg`, `probe`, `end`, `restart`), replayed in order through the interface in `pilot/assistant.py`. Only probe replies are graded: an LLM judge checks each one against the probe's `expect.should` and `expect.should_not`. `python -m eval.scenario` lints every file (0 problems), and `python -m eval.run --config eval/config.json` replays them all against the five variants (see the README).

## Why each suite exists

- **`samples/`**: reconstructions of the brief's three attached samples, which we never received (ASSUMPTIONS.md, A1). Each follows the brief's one-line description of its sample, and `sample_01` contains the brief's excerpt lines word for word, in their original order. Results on these files describe our reconstructions, not the graders' originals.
- **`holdout/`**: scenarios not used for tuning (ASSUMPTIONS A1 lists where development still saw them). `sib_01` to `sib_03` are paraphrased siblings of the three samples: the same shape with new people and new wording, and no names or signature phrases taken from the samples. `gs_01` to `gs_04` are grader-style scenarios, written the way a grader might write the hidden ones, each with a trap for a plausible shortcut.
- **`own/`**: our own scenarios. `own_01` to `own_05` each reproduce one complaint (2 to 6; complaint 1 is covered by sample_01, sib_01, gs_01, long_01 and the sweep) and `own_06` a replaced fact. `own_07` and `own_08` test two of the brief's interface rules: two users never share information, and a skipped `end_session` or a restart loses nothing. `own_09` to `own_12` are aimed at four weaknesses we expect in our own design.
- **`long/`**: six weeks of realistic use, generated deterministically by `python -m eval.longgen` from a written message pool (`long/_pool/`). One fact is planted early and asked about at the end, far outside b0's window; `long_04` is one heavy day instead.
- **`sweep/`**: the same preference behind 0 to 320 messages of filler, to show where b0's window stops reaching it and how input tokens grow with the history. `sweep_00` to `sweep_40` are hand-written; `sweep_080`, `sweep_160` and `sweep_320` extend `sweep_40` with pool messages and are generated by `eval/longgen.py`.

## How to read the tables

- **Variants.** v0 = current Pilot (system prompt and the current session only). b0 = the brief's baseline (system prompt and as much of the newest history across sessions as fits under the 8,000-token cap; it fills to a 7,400-token target). b1 = b0 plus our grounding rules. lex = our design without the extractor (message log, keyword search, today's earlier messages and the session tail). new = the full design (lex plus validated account notes, shown as account cards).
- **Complaints** are numbered as the six user quotes in the brief.
- **Expected winner** is a prediction, not a result: P = expected pass, F = expected fail, ? = could go either way. For the probes listed in `eval/PREDICTIONS.md`, which was written before any lex or new replay existed, the letters are copied from it. It does not list the `gs_*`, `long_*` and `sweep_080` to `sweep_320` probes; their predictions were written with the files. Each run's `results/runs/<tag>/report.md` has the results.
- **b0's reach.** The "~N tokens back" figures in the hand-written rows are pre-run estimates that assumed 45 to 120 tokens per acknowledgement and 450 to 700 per draft or prep reply. In the exploratory baseline run (`results/runs/baseline`), b0's replies averaged about 71 tokens and its prompt never filled: on every probe that run covered except own_02's, the needed fact was still in b0's prompt, and in b1's. So wherever a row expects b0 to fail or wobble because the fact has left its window (sample_01, sample_02, sib_01, sib_02, sweep_40, own_01, own_08, own_10), that reason did not hold in that run, and the probe tests what b0 does with the fact in view. The `long/` files and `sweep_080` to `sweep_320` exist because of this: there the fact is 1.5 to 7.3 times b0's budget away. The gs_* files came after that run; their b0 figures use the method of the last section (about 71 tokens per reply).
- **Needles and audit controls.** `scenarios/needles.json` lists short phrases from the fact each probe depends on (an empty list for "not on record" probes). The eval records whether they were in the exact prompt sent to the model, which separates a retrieval miss from a reasoning miss. `eval/audit_controls.jsonl` holds a known-good and a known-bad reply for every probe; `python -m eval.audit` uses them to check the judge.
- **Shared conventions.** Timestamps fall on weekdays in 2026 between 08:00 and 18:30 and strictly increase per user. Every session begins with `start`, and some sessions have no `end` (the user closed the tab). A `restart` comes at every day boundary and between some sessions within a day. Each probe's `should_not` names the look-alikes that sit in the history (another account's date, contact, price or cadence), so the judge can check a reply on its own; no probe depends on a trick word.

## Long-horizon scenarios (generated by eval/longgen.py)

**Why these exist.** Almost every hand-written scenario fits inside b0's recency window (own_02's 13k-token paste is the exception), so none of them can show the brief's "gets less useful the more they use it". b0's real replies are short: the 525 b0 replies in `results/runs/baseline` average 70.8 tokens, far below the 450-700 tokens per draft that the pre-run estimates above assumed. With replies that short, b0's prompt at the sweep_40 probe was only 5,224 tokens, so the whole history fits and b0 sees the needle. (The estimate below gives 5,505 for that prompt, +5%; every file below needs at least 1.5x b0's budget, a far wider margin than that error.) These files replay six weeks of daily use, so the fact a probe needs ends up far outside the window.

**How they are built** (`python -m eval.longgen`; no randomness, so re-running rewrites the same bytes):
- Source: the message pool in `long/_pool/`: 313 messages on 12 accounts over workdays 1-30 (Mon 2 Feb 2026 to Fri 13 Mar 2026), about 10 a day (66 draft, 192 log, 37 prep, 18 question). `long_04` uses `heavy_day.json` alone (71 messages on Tue 10 Feb 2026).
- Sessions: within a day, a message joins the current session while it comes at most 25 minutes after the previous one and the session has fewer than 4 messages. `start` is 1 minute before the first message and `end` 2 minutes after the last; every 4th session has no `end` (the tab was closed). A `restart` separates days. Time only moves forward: an event that would not come after the previous one moves to one minute after it, so a few messages are sent a minute or two after their pool time (at most 3 minutes; see the last column of the stats table).
- Needles are their own sessions at their stated day and time, moved 5 minutes later until they overlap no pool session. The probes come in one session on Mon 16 Mar 2026, after a restart (`long_04`: 18:05 the same day).
- The pool never mentions a planted account or person (`eval/longgen.py` checks this on every run), so each probe has one right answer.
- Sweeps: `sweep_080/160/320` keep sweep_40 byte for byte up to its probe session, append the first 40/120/280 pool messages from the next workday (session ids continue from s22), then repeat sweep_40's probe session (same text and expect) on the next workday at 15:01. The pool's texts keep their own Feb-Mar dates, and some of its accounts (Contoso, Northwind, Tailspin, ...) also appear in sweep_40 with other contacts. As filler that is harmless: the probe is about Fabrikam, which the pool never mentions.

**b0 reach** = the tokens b0 would have to carry from the needle message up to the probe: user turns exactly as b0 sends them (with its time stamp; Qwen3 tokenizer), plus 70.8 tokens per reply (the measured mean) and 14 tokens of chat template per message pair. The budget is b0's 7,400-token target minus its system prompt, the probe and 10 tokens of prompt overhead (7,140-7,155 here). Outside = the needle cannot be in b0's prompt. At this pool's average of 166 tokens per message pair, the budget holds about 42 pairs, about 4.0 workdays of the pool. These are estimates; the eval's `needle_hits` (in each probe row of the events) measures the real reach.
